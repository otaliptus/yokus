## 2. teahouse-peshrev-1912.mp3
- Title: Columbia E1131 #38310 (the matrix is listed in the Discography of American Historical Recordings as "Husseini pashrav", that is, a Hüseyni peşrev)
- Artist: M. G. Parsekian and Company (per DAHR); the archive.org item credits "Columbia Record"
- Source page: https://archive.org/details/columbia-e-1131-38310
- Direct file: https://archive.org/download/columbia-e-1131-38310/Columbia%20E1131%20-%2038310.mp3
- Licence on the page: "Public Domain Mark 1.0" (licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/)
- Why the recording itself is public domain: DAHR lists this disc (Columbia E1131, matrices 38310/38311) as recorded 7 Oct 1912 and released 1913. US recordings published before 1923 entered the public domain on 1 Jan 2022 under the Music Modernization Act. The phonogram term in Turkey and the EU ended long ago, and the composition is traditional Ottoman.
- Page description: "78 RPM 10" shellac phonograph record from the 'church cache'. Digitized on Audio-Technica AT120XUSB ... Attempted cleanup of audio with isotope plugins." Subjects: shellac, 78RPM, music, turkish, armenian. Language: tur.
- Duration: 3:05 (184.6 s)
- Mood and use: an old Ottoman instrumental prelude with shellac crackle, like an old record playing in the village tea house.

## Notes and caveats
- None of the tracks were listened to, because there is no audio decoder on this machine. The moods above come from
  the page descriptions, tags and titles. Audition them before you commit to any of them.
- Files 2 and 3 are acoustic-era 78 rpm transfers. They are mono-ish and crackly, and they are not seamless loops. Use them as
  in-world records or radio, not as clean background music. The tempo of the 1916 medley is unknown and it may be livelier than ideal.
- The DAHR date and title for file 2 come from a search-engine result for the DAHR record (adp.library.ucsb.edu objects/190387).
  The DAHR page itself returned HTTP 403, so the title "Husseini pashrav" could not be checked directly.
- The OGA tracks are game-music renders (VST/LMMS/notation software), not live ethnic instruments. Only #1 has an explicit
  "desert/Eastern" flavour among the modern tracks.
